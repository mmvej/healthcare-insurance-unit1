Records (n)
The number of rows in the selected group, not a verified count of unique people. Matching rows are retained because there is no ID proving that they describe the same person. Each selected row contributes 1. In the equations below, xi is the charge for row i, ∑ means add across all selected rows, and x̄ is their mean.
n = ∑i=1n 1

Mean (x̄)
The arithmetic average: add all selected charges and divide by their count. Large charges can pull it upwards. It is undefined when n = 0.
x̄ = (x1 + ⋯ + xn) / n = ∑ xi / n

Median
The middle charge after sorting from lowest to highest. Write the sorted charges as x(1), …, x(n); the number in brackets is the position, not the original row number. It is undefined for an empty group.
Odd n: median = x((n+1)/2)
Even n: median = [x(n/2) + x(n/2+1)] / 2

Sample standard deviation (SD or s)
The spread around the mean, calculated from squared distances. A larger SD means greater spread. The dashboard uses the sample formula, dividing by n − 1. SD has the same units as charges (displayed as USD under the stated assumption). It is undefined for fewer than two records.
s = √[∑ (xi − x̄)2 / (n − 1)]

Quartiles and interquartile range (IQR)
Q1 is the 25th percentile and Q3 the 75th percentile: the cut-offs at the lower and upper quarter positions in the sorted charges. The IQR measures the width of the middle 50% and is less affected by extremes than SD. Percentiles use linear interpolation between sorted values.
Q1 = Q(0.25), Q3 = Q(0.75)
IQR = Q3 − Q1

Association and Pearson correlation (r)
An association is a pattern in how variables vary together. Pearson r measures the direction and strength of a linear association. Here x is age or BMI and y is charges; x̄ and ȳ are their selected-group means. r ranges from −1 (perfect downward linear relationship) to +1 (perfect upward linear relationship). Near 0 means little linear relationship; a curved pattern may still exist. If either variable has no variation, r is undefined. The dashboard also withholds r for fewer than three records. Association does not establish cause and effect.
r = ∑[(xi − x̄)(yi − ȳ)] / √[∑(xi − x̄)2 × ∑(yi − ȳ)2]

Skewness
A numerical measure of asymmetry. Positive values indicate asymmetry towards higher charges; negative values indicate asymmetry towards lower charges. Values near zero suggest limited asymmetry, but can also occur with multiple peaks. The dashboard uses the adjusted sample skewness from pandas. Descriptions use ±0.5 as a rough guide to direction, not a significance test.

Excess kurtosis
A measure based on fourth-power deviations from the mean, sensitive to extreme values relative to the standard deviation. The normal-distribution reference is 0: positive values indicate more weight in extreme deviations; negative values indicate less. This is not a measure of peak height or proof of normality. The dashboard uses pandas’ sample-adjusted Fisher excess kurtosis, requiring at least four records.

Conclusion

Across all 1,338 records, smoking status shows a substantial difference in medical insurance costs: the smoking group has a mean about 3.8 times that of non-smokers. Age is positively associated with expenditure, while the relationship between BMI and medical costs is much stronger among smokers than non-smokers. Regional rankings change depending on whether mean, median or smoking groups are compared. Sex and numbers of children do not support a simple, consistent pattern of higher costs.

For planning, the main lesson is to compare groups and distributions rather than rely on one overall average. Use both mean and median, check group sizes, and investigate differences before making budget decisions.
